What makes design as a discipline unique?

Tuan Huang
2026-06-08
Design Education x AI Pilot

Highlights & Summary

In this session, participants discussed what makes design uniquely resilient to AI, which landed on three qualities: its integration across disciplines, its training of self-directed learners, and its capacity to hold tensions rather than flatten them into clean solutions.

Activity Format

This was a plenary session when participants came back from two breakout rooms that shared positive stories and anecdotes on AI and also how students were currently using AI in the educators’ classrooms.

Bringing these together, we discussed around the question: what makes design discipline unique and if there are some qualities in design that can make this discipline resilient to AI presence.

This plenary session was 30 minute, joined by all participants.

Outcomes

  • Some unique qualities in design and design education that may have potential to be resilient to AI;
    • Design focuses on integration across disciplines, working with other fields of people and the skill of integration and agility make it harder to be replaced by AI
    • Since there’s no single route for design problems, each situated in its unique context and people, students are trained with self-directed learning, to figure out a path. And this self-directed learning can become a lifelong skill to navigate through the digital scenes, and pick up new knowledge and skills as needed in the future
    • Design works within a space of tensions, and intentionally hold the tensions - whether between human and technology, between aesthetic and efficiency - designers are trained to cope with messy and wicked problems that may not always be clean and consistent, but to generate new and constructive experiences out of it. This contrasts to some other disciplines where they focus more on smoothing out tensions. It also contrasts to AI’s general behavior, to be monoaesthetic and clean. Holding the tensions also implies a sense of criticality, understanding the stakes and people involved, the consequences of design decisions. However, we are losing both the criticality and tension when AI is involved, as it tends to overshoot the problems and reduce it to something too simple. In response, facilitations and critiques in classrooms should be updated and reinforced.
