What makes design as a discipline unique?

Tuan Huang
2026-06-08
Design Education x AI Pilot

Highlights & Summary

In this session, participants discussed what makes design uniquely resilient to AI, which landed on three qualities: its integration across disciplines, its training of self-directed learners, and its capacity to hold tensions rather than flatten them into clean solutions.

Activity Format

A 30-minute plenary session bringing all participants together after two breakout rooms. Groups had shared positive stories and anecdotes about AI, as well as observations about how students were currently using it in the classroom. The plenary used these as a springboard to discuss a shared question: what makes design as a discipline unique, and are there qualities within it that could make it resilient to AI?

Outcomes

Three qualities emerged as potentially distinctive to design and design education:

Integration and agility

Design works across disciplines, with diverse people and contexts. The skill of integrating across fields — and adapting as those fields shift — is difficult to replicate or replace.

Self-directed learning

Because design problems have no single solution, students are trained to find their own path. This becomes a transferable, lifelong capacity to navigate unfamiliar terrain and acquire new knowledge as needed.

Holding tensions

Designers are trained to work within mess and contradiction, between human and technology, aesthetic and efficiency, rather than resolving it prematurely. This contrasts both with disciplines that smooth tensions over and with AI’s tendency toward monoaesthetic, reductive outputs. Holding tension implies criticality: understanding stakes, people, and consequences of decisions. The concern raised is that AI use in design risks eroding this quality, and that facilitation and critique in design classrooms may need to be actively reinforced in response.
